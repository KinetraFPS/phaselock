What the research says

The methods here, and how strong the evidence behind each one is.

Neural AM · strongest for focus

Fast amplitude modulation in music helped sustained attention

Brain.fm's research team, with Psyche Loui's lab, embedded beta-range (around 16 Hz) amplitude modulation in music. Listeners with more ADHD symptoms performed better on a sustained-attention task, and fMRI showed changes in attention networks. This is the method behind the Focus programs.

Woods, Loui et al., Communications Biology, 2024 Read the paper ↗
Binaural beats · mixed

Measurable effects on anxiety and some cognition, weak evidence for true entrainment

A 2019 meta-analysis found binaural beats reduced anxiety and modestly helped memory and attention, especially with exposure before a task. A 2023 systematic review found EEG evidence of actual brainwave entrainment inconsistent. Beats are clearest with carriers of 200–500 Hz and rates below about 30 Hz.

Garcia-Argibay et al., Psychological Research, 2019 · Ingendoh et al., PLOS ONE, 2023 Read the review ↗
40 Hz gamma · early

40 Hz sensory stimulation is an active research area

MIT's Tsai lab found 40 Hz light and sound reduced Alzheimer's-related markers in mice, and human trials are under way. Effects on attention in healthy listeners are not established. Gamma 40 uses amplitude modulation, since binaural beats fade above about 30 Hz.

Iaccarino et al., Nature, 2016 · Martorell et al., Cell, 2019 Read the study ↗

Phaselock is a sound tool, not a medical device. Keep the volume moderate. Skip isochronic pulses if you have a seizure disorder, and don't use sleep programs while driving. The scope never flashes at the beat rate.
